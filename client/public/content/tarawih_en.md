## Meaning of Taraweeh Prayer

**Taraweeh** (التراويح): Its original meaning is moving from rest to rest. This name comes from the tradition of resting after every four rak’ahs. Taraweeh prayer is a confirmed Sunnah for both men and women. Its status as a confirmed Sunnah is unanimously agreed upon by the Companions _r.a._ and prominent scholars of later generations.

According to the Hanafi school, a person who rejects this confirmed Sunnah is considered a _bid’ati_ (مبتدع - an innovator in religion), and their testimony is not trusted. This view is based on the Prophet’s _sallallahu alayhi wa sallam_ sayings:

> "Allah has prescribed for you Qiyam (القيام - night prayer) in the nights of Ramadan as a Sunnah"

and

> "Follow my Sunnah and the way of the rightly-guided caliphs after me"

In the Hanafi school, the words and actions of the _Caliphs_ (الخلفاء - caliphs, the leaders of Muslims after the Prophet) are a source of Sunnah.

## Historical Background

As mentioned in Hadith 731 of the _Bukhari_ collection, the Prophet _sallallahu alayhi wa sallam_ performed Taraweeh prayer with his Companions _r.a._ for two or four nights during Ramadan. The reason he did not perform it regularly is explained—he feared it might become obligatory (واجب) for his Ummah.

After the Prophet _sallallahu alayhi wa sallam_ stopped leading Taraweeh in congregation, Muslims continued performing it individually until the caliphate of Umar ibn al-Khattab (_عمر بن الخطاب - r.a._). Umar _r.a._ ordered Ubay ibn Kaab (_أبي بن كعب_) to lead Taraweeh in congregation. All Companions _r.a._, including great Companions like Ali _r.a._, supported this initiative. Ali _r.a._ praised Umar _r.a._, saying:

> "نور الله مضجع عمر كما نور مساجدنا"  
> (May Allah illuminate Umar’s resting place as He illuminated our mosques)

Some scholars consider Taraweeh prayer _mustahab_ (مستحب - recommended), but the most correct opinion is that it is a _confirmed Sunnah_ (سنة مؤكدة).

## Prayer Timing

Taraweeh prayer is performed every Ramadan night after the Isha prayer’s obligatory and Sunnah parts, but before the Witr prayer. The time for Taraweeh extends from after Isha prayer until dawn.

- If someone prays Isha without ablution and then performs ablution to pray Taraweeh, they must repeat both Isha and Taraweeh, as Taraweeh’s time only begins after the obligatory Isha prayer.
- Some Bukharan scholars consider Taraweeh’s time to be from nightfall until dawn, allowing it to be prayed before or after Isha, as it is described as "night prayer" (قيام الليل). However, the majority of scholars’ strong opinion is that Taraweeh must be prayed after Isha and before Witr. Thus, if someone prays Taraweeh before Isha or after Witr, it does not count as Taraweeh.

## Praying in Congregation

Praying Taraweeh in congregation is a _Sunnah kifayah_ (سنة كفاية - a Sunnah sufficient if some perform it). This means:

- If a sufficient number of people perform it in congregation, the obligation is lifted from the rest of the Muslims (like the funeral prayer or memorizing the Quran).
- However, if no one prays it in congregation at the mosque, the entire community is considered sinful.

Someone who prays Taraweeh in congregation at home but does not go to the mosque misses one merit (going to the mosque) but gains another (praying in congregation).

## Order of Taraweeh Prayer

Excluding Witr, Taraweeh prayer consists of **20 rak’ahs**. This is:

- Performed with 10 salams, meaning a salam is given after every two rak’ahs.
- It is also correct to give a salam after every four rak’ahs.
- Alternatively, the four rak’ahs can be performed with one salam.

Resting after every four rak’ahs for the duration it took to pray those four rak’ahs is a Sunnah. Traditionally:

- The people of Mecca performed _tawaf_ (الطواف - circumambulation of the Kaaba) after every four rak’ahs.
- The people of Medina recited _salawat_ (الصلاة - blessings) on the Prophet _sallallahu alayhi wa sallam_ during this time.
- People in other regions prayed, recited _tasbihat_ (التسبيحات - glorifications), or simply sat quietly.
